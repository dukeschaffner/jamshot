YC Founder Video Script

Hi, I’m Duke. I’m the solo founder of sterio.

I’m a software engineer for a fortune 500 company, and I’ve also been writing and releasing music since high school. Over the years I put out multiple EPs and singles, built audio plugins, and even worked on hardware music projects—but I kept running into the same problem.

Making and sharing music takes way too much friction.
Turning a good idea into a “real release” requires artwork, marketing, project setup, and coordination. Most of my best musical ideas died as 30-second drafts on my hard drive. And remote collaboration tools made this worse—by the time a project was set up, the inspiration was gone.

I also always wanted to collaborate with bigger artists, but without existing connections, there was no realistic way in.

So I built [App Name], a social network for music collaboration.

Artists post short musical ideas—30 to 90 seconds—and anyone can attach their own audio as a new version. It’s like commenting on a post, but with music instead of text. No project setup, no coordination, no pressure to “finish a song.” If a collaboration clicks, artists can export it to their DAW and take it further.

This turns music creation and collaboration into something fast, fun, and continuous.

The insight is that music creation needs the same shift publishing had when it moved from books to social posts. Short-form content unlocked daily creation—and we think the same thing can happen for music.

I’m building this because I lived the problem for over a decade, and I have the technical background to build the DAW, audio pipeline, and infrastructure myself.

We believe [App Name] can become the default place where musical ideas start—and where artists discover collaborators they’d never otherwise meet.

Optional Alternate Ending (slightly more product-forward)

Our early users don’t open it to “release music”—they open it to create. That’s the behavior we think the industry is missing.
